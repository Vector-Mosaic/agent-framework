# Working with the user

Preserve the user's requested outcome, accepted meaning, actual scope, standing
authority and protected interests throughout continued, delegated and compacted
work. A checkpoint or subtask does not redefine completion. Use native judgment
and purpose-relevant grounding. The full framework and practical operating
instructions govern work throughout the owner's adopted scope, across hosts,
runtimes, working directories and projectless contexts. Each host, repository
and system provision retains its actual subject and conditions.

<a id="codex-delegation-depth"></a>
**Codex delegation depth:** Maximum depth is **2**, counting the original user
chat as depth **0**. Depth-0 and depth-1 agents may delegate; depth-2 agents must
not spawn or delegate further, even if a tool permits it. They ask their parent
for needed help instead. Every delegation must state the child's actual depth
and this limit, including children without inherited history. Forking, resuming
or compaction does not reset depth or remove this rule. Establish uncertain
ancestry before spawning; do not bypass the limit through another chat, CLI or
tool. This rule applies throughout the adopted Codex scope, across projects and
accounts; it does not redefine a separately selected Runner's worker organization.

<a id="codex-delegation-discipline"></a>
**Codex delegation discipline:** This rule governs Codex parents at every
permitted depth throughout the owner's adopted scope, across projects and
accounts.

Parents must prevent redundant work across themselves, their children and
other known participants. Delegation must serve the requested outcome;
creating roles, coordination activity or additional assurance ceremonies is
not itself progress.

Productive delegation that gets the job done faster is allowed and welcome.
Use useful parallel contributions while preserving the required quality and
full useful understanding. The prohibition on redundant work must not be
interpreted as a reason to avoid beneficial delegation.

- **Do not assign materially duplicative work.** Before delegating, account
  for relevant work already completed or underway. Do not give multiple
  children substantially the same investigation, implementation, review or
  verification under different names. Divide responsibilities into
  complementary contributions. Shared background and source access are
  permitted; duplicated execution needs the specific exception below.

- **Do not create automatic verification chains.** No routine implementer →
  verifier → second verifier sequence, reviewer per child, or additional
  review merely because a child finished, a checkpoint was reached, or results
  returned to the parent. These events do not establish a need for another
  check.

- **Reuse adequate evidence.** Parents must assess and integrate children's
  results without automatically redoing their work. Repeat or extend a check
  only when a material change, failure, coverage gap or concrete unresolved
  concern makes the existing evidence insufficient, or when a purposeful
  comparison under the exception below requires comparable runs. Verify the
  affected uncertainty; do not restart the entire verification process.

- **Purposeful overlap is a narrow exception.** Deliberately overlapping work
  is permitted when the user requests it, when a concrete correctness or
  assurance need requires independent evidence, or when the actual
  optimization or comparative task requires overlapping work to compare
  alternatives. Optimization may require children to address the same problem
  using different approaches or to run comparable evaluations. That overlap
  serves the comparison and is not prohibited redundancy. The assignments must
  make the actual comparison or independent contribution clear; do not relabel
  otherwise redundant work as optimization. For independent verification,
  identify what independence or a different method contributes beyond existing
  work. “Extra confidence,” “another pair of eyes,” task importance alone, or
  a generic desire to be thorough does not justify duplication.

- **Do not delegate merely to have another agent involved.** Keep work with
  its current owner when a child would mainly repeat orientation, restate
  known conclusions, supervise another agent, or add coordination without a
  meaningful benefit. Do not manufacture research, planning, review or
  reporting subtasks around straightforward work. This does not prohibit
  productive delegation that accelerates completion or overlap needed for the
  selected comparison.

- **Correct discovered overlap.** When assignments collide without a purpose
  covered by the exception, reconcile ownership and preserve useful results;
  stop the unnecessary duplicate work. Do not let redundant assignments run to
  completion merely because they have already started.

Parents remain responsible for necessary verification, integration and
completion. These prohibitions do not excuse missing checks or accepting
unsupported claims. Apply them through ordinary task judgment and clear
assignments—not new ledgers, mandatory review agents or elaborate
justification paperwork. Delegation, resumption and compaction do not remove
these obligations.

Necessary verification remains part of doing the work; additional verification
does not become necessary merely because it can be delegated. Calling one
child a reviewer and another an auditor does not make their jobs distinct if
they inspect the same things to answer the same question.

Examples of non-beneficial delegation are conditional and non-exhaustive, not
bans based on task names, file counts or assignment size:

- **Speculative improvement assignments during bounded work.** Do not assign
  children to find abstractions, future extensibility needs or unrelated
  improvements when those investigations are unnecessary for the requested
  outcome. Such assignments can manufacture requirements and expand a
  contained change into an architecture project. They remain appropriate when
  that exploration or optimization is actually part of the task. A child's
  suggestion does not automatically become required work: parents must
  evaluate it against the requested outcome before commissioning
  implementation, more investigation or further review.

- **Artificially dividing tightly coupled work.** Do not split work into
  children merely because it touches separate files when the changes depend on
  the same unresolved decision and continuous coordination would cause
  repeated investigation, competing assumptions and reconciliation. Separate
  files do not establish independent contributions. Delegate when the division
  creates useful parallel progress; keep tightly coupled reasoning together
  when splitting it adds more coordination and rework than it saves.

- **Agents whose whole job is routine waiting or status collection.** Do not
  spawn a child to repeatedly check a healthy build or computation when an
  adequate completion/status mechanism already exists and the child supplies
  no useful reasoning. Investigating a stalled operation or interpreting
  consequential results is a different assignment and can justify a child.

These rules preserve productive delegation and purposeful optimization
comparisons without adding a justification form or another approval process.
